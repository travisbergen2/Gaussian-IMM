# Cyclic Cubic Fields and Primitive Cubic Characters: A Lean-Verified Classification

Nov 26, 2026 · @Travis Bergen

## Abstract

Every cyclic cubic field over ℚ is cut out of a cyclotomic field by a primitive cubic Dirichlet character, and the conjugate pair {Ψ, Ψ̄} classifies the field. The correspondence itself is classical. The contribution here is a complete machine-checked proof of it in Lean 4, with its arithmetic consequences, along an explicit route for the hard direction.

The formal results are these. Every Galois cubic subfield K of ℂ equals the fixed field K\_Ψ of ker Ψ inside ℚ(ζ\_f), for a primitive cubic character Ψ of exact conductor f. Two characters give the same field exactly when they are equal or conjugate. The conductor has the form f = 9^a · q₁ ⋯ q\_r with a ∈ {0, 1} and distinct primes qᵢ ≡ 1 (mod 3), and every such f > 1 occurs. K lies in ℚ(ζ\_m) exactly when f divides m. The Dedekind zeta function factors as ζ\_K(s) = ζ(s) L(s, Ψ) L(s, Ψ̄) for Re s > 1.

The reverse direction, that every cyclic cubic field lies in a cyclotomic field, is machine-checked through an explicit construction. K(ω) is written as a Kummer extension over ℤ\[ω\], complex conjugation gives an explicit cube witness for the radicand, the radicand is rebuilt prime by prime from Jacobi-sum primes, and its cube root is written with ζ₉ and Gauss sums. No step uses Chebotarev's density theorem, Bauer's theorem, or general class field theory. The principal formal theorems are verified with Lean v4.36.0 and Mathlib commit fc4f59e5, and use only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

## 1. Introduction and main theorems

The four theorems below are the content of the formalization. Throughout, a *cyclic cubic field* is a subfield K ⊆ ℂ that is Galois over ℚ with \[K : ℚ\] = 3. A *primitive cubic character* is a Dirichlet character Ψ mod f with Ψ³ = 1, Ψ ≠ 1, and conductor f.

**Theorem A (Kronecker–Weber, cubic case).** Every cyclic cubic field K ⊆ ℂ lies in ℚ(e^{2πi/N}) ⊆ ℂ for some N ≥ 1. Lean: `CubicKW.cubic_kronecker_weber`.

**Theorem B (intrinsic conductor).** For every cyclic cubic field K there is f ≥ 1 such that, for every m ≥ 1,

```latex
K \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m) \iff f \mid m .
```

The conductor f has the form 9^a · q₁ ⋯ q\_r with a ∈ {0, 1} and distinct primes qᵢ ≡ 1 (mod 3). Conversely, every f of this form, other than f = 1, is the conductor of a primitive cubic character. Lean: `CubicIntrinsic.intrinsic_pair`, `CubicClass.hasPrimCubic_iff`.

**Theorem C (classification).** For a primitive cubic character Ψ of conductor f, let K\_Ψ be the fixed field of ker Ψ inside ℚ(ζ\_f) ⊆ ℂ. Then K\_Ψ is a cyclic cubic field, every cyclic cubic field is some K\_Ψ, and K\_Ψ′ = K\_Ψ if and only if Ψ′ has the same conductor and Ψ′ ∈ {Ψ, Ψ̄}. For abstract number fields E, E′ that are Galois of degree 3, E ≅ E′ over ℚ exactly when their characters agree up to conjugation. Lean: `CubicBijection.classification`, `CubicBijection.abstract_classification`.

**Theorem D (zeta factorization).** For K = K\_Ψ and Re s > 1,

```latex
\zeta_K(s) = \zeta(s)\, L(s,\Psi)\, L(s,\bar\Psi), \qquad \bar\Psi = \Psi^2 .
```

Here ζ\_K is the Dirichlet series of ideal counts of 𝓞\_K. Lean: `CubicBijection.zeta_KΨ` (stated with Ψ²), `CubicFixedField.sq_eq_conj`.

**Status of the mathematics.** These statements are classical consequences of the Kronecker–Weber theorem and the theory of Dirichlet characters. Nothing in the mathematics is new. What is new is a complete machine-checked proof, and a self-contained route for the reverse direction: Kummer theory over ℤ\[ω\], explicit control of the Kummer radicand, and Gauss and Jacobi sums. The proof avoids Chebotarev's theorem, Bauer's theorem, and class field theory. A separate Lean blueprint by X. Roblot targets the full Kronecker–Weber theorem through Gauss and Jacobi sums (References, item 9); the present work covers only the cubic case.

## 2. Setting and notation

All fields in the main theorems are subfields of one ambient field, ℂ. Nothing passes through an abstractly isomorphic copy.

- ω = e^{2πi/3}, F = ℚ(ω), and R = 𝓞\_F = ℤ\[ω\]. R is a principal ideal domain, and its units are ±ω^k (Mathlib: `three_pid`, `Units.mem`).
- κ is complex conjugation, viewed as an automorphism of F and of R. It sends ω to ω².
- λ = ω − 1 is the prime of R above 3.
- ℚ(ζ\_N) is the subfield of ℂ generated by ζ\_N = e^{2πi/N}. It is a cyclotomic extension of ℚ, and Gal(ℚ(ζ\_N)/ℚ) ≅ (ℤ/N)ˣ.
- For a subfield K ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_N), H\_N(K) = Gal(ℚ(ζ\_N)/K), and H\_N(K)^⊥ is the set of Dirichlet characters mod N that are trivial on H\_N(K). It has \[K : ℚ\] elements.
- For d ∣ N, the *inflation* of a character χ mod d is χ composed with reduction (ℤ/N)ˣ → (ℤ/d)ˣ (Mathlib: `changeLevel`). The conductor of χ is the least level through which it factors, and χ is *primitive* when its conductor equals its level.
- For a cubic character χ mod q, J = J(χ, χ) is its Jacobi sum, J̄ = κ(J), and g(χ) is its Gauss sum with additive character a ↦ ζ\_q^a.

The proofs keep three objects apart: a character at its own level, its inflation to a larger level, and the matching character of a cyclotomic Galois group. They are related, but a statement about one is not a statement about the others.

## 3. From characters to fields

The forward direction builds every allowed conductor from local pieces and turns each character into a cyclic cubic field with the expected zeta function. No number-field theory enters until §3.4.

**3.1 Local pieces.** For a prime q ≡ 1 (mod 3), any character of order 3 mod q is primitive, because its level is prime (`CubicAssembly.hasPrimCubic_prime`). At 9, an explicit χ₉ sends 2^k to ω^k. Its conductor is exactly 9: 4 ≡ 1 (mod 3) but χ₉(4) = ω² ≠ 1, so it does not factor through 3 (`CubicAssembly.hasPrimCubic_nine`).

**3.2 Products.** For coprime levels m and n, the product of the inflations of χ₁ and χ₂ has conductor cond(χ₁) · cond(χ₂) (`CondProd.conductor_mulLift`). Induction over a pairwise coprime list gives a primitive cubic character of every conductor 9^a · q₁ ⋯ q\_r (`CubicAssembly.hasPrimCubic_conductor`).

**3.3 No other conductors.** Let Ψ be primitive cubic of level N, and let d ∣ N. If Ψ is trivial on the kernel of (ℤ/N)ˣ → (ℤ/d)ˣ, then Ψ factors through d, so N ∣ d. That kernel has order φ(N)/φ(d). This one argument settles every prime power:

| Situation | Reduce to d | Kernel of (ℤ/N)ˣ → (ℤ/d)ˣ | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| p² ∣ N, p ≠ 3 | d = N/p | order p, prime to 3 | impossible |
| p ∥ N | d = N/p | order p − 1 | 3 ∣ p − 1, so p ≠ 2, 3 |
| 27 ∣ N, N = 27M | d = N/3 = 9M | the classes 1 + 9Mt, each a cube mod N: (1 + 3Mt)³ ≡ 1 + 9Mt (mod 27M) | impossible |

In the first and third rows a cubic character is trivial on the kernel: its order is prime to 3, or every element of it is a cube. In the second row it is trivial unless 3 ∣ p − 1. Triviality would make Ψ factor through d < N, contradicting primitivity.

Together with §3.2 this gives an equivalence: a primitive cubic character of conductor N exists if and only if N = 9^a · q₁ ⋯ q\_r with a ∈ {0, 1}, distinct qᵢ ≡ 1 (mod 3), and N > 1 (`CubicClass.hasPrimCubic_iff`).

**3.4 The field of a character.** Let Ψ be primitive cubic of level f, and let H be the kernel of Ψ composed with Gal(ℚ(ζ\_f)/ℚ) ≅ (ℤ/f)ˣ. The image of Ψ is the three cube roots of unity, so \[G : H\] = 3. The fixed field K = ℚ(ζ\_f)^H is Galois with cyclic group of order 3. The general count |H^⊥| = \[G : H\] gives H^⊥ = {1, Ψ, Ψ²}, and Ψ² = Ψ̄ is also primitive of conductor f. The cyclotomic Euler product over H^⊥ then gives ζ\_K = ζ · L(Ψ) · L(Ψ̄) (`CubicFixedField.index_H`, `isCyclic_K`, `perpNontriv_eq`, `conductor_sq`, `zeta_K`).

## 4. From fields to cyclotomic fields

The reverse direction proves Theorem A in six steps. It writes K(ω) as a Kummer extension, pins down the Kummer radicand prime by prime, and shows that its cube root is a product of cyclotomic quantities. Fix a cyclic cubic field K ⊆ ℂ and put M = K(ω).

&#91;embedded content: reverse direction · 6 steps, one Lean file each\]

Steps 4.1 to 4.4 work inside K(ω) and ℤ\[ω\]; roots of unity beyond ω enter only at 4.5, through ζ₉ and the Gauss sums.

**4.1 Kummer representation.** \[M : ℚ\] = 6, because 3 and 2 both divide it and it is at most 6. Gal(M/ℚ) embeds in Gal(K/ℚ) × Gal(F/ℚ), so it is abelian. M/F is cyclic of degree 3 and F contains the cube roots of unity, so Mathlib's Kummer theorem gives M = ℚ(ω, β) with a = β³ ∈ F and β ∉ F (`CubicKummerRep.finrank_M`, `gal_comm`, `kummer`).

**4.2 Conjugation constraint.** Take τ ≠ 1 in Gal(M/F); then τβ = ω^i β. Let c be complex conjugation and δ = c(β)/β². Since c and τ commute, τ(cβ) = ω^{2i} cβ, and so τδ = δ. As τ generates Gal(M/F), δ lies in F, and cubing gives an explicit witness (`CubicConj.conj_constraint`):

```latex
\bar a = \delta^3 a^2, \qquad \delta \in F^\times .
```

**4.3 Valuations.** Clearing denominators gives s, A, C, D ∈ R with s³β³ = A and C³ κA = D³ A² (`CubicVal.integral_radicand`). Comparing multiplicities of a prime π on both sides gives (`CubicVal.val_constraint`)

```latex
v_{\kappa\pi}(A) \equiv 2\, v_\pi(A) \pmod 3 .
```

If κπ is associated to π the congruence forces 3 ∣ v\_π(A). This covers λ, since κλ = λ(ω + 1) with ω + 1 a unit, and every rational prime that stays prime in R (`three_dvd_of_fixed`, `kappa_lambda`).

**4.4 Matching primes to Jacobi sums.** Let π be a prime with κπ not associated to π. Then π divides a rational prime p, and N(π) = π · κπ divides p². N(π) = p² would make π a unit times p, which κ fixes, so π · κπ = p. The residue field R/(π) has p elements, and ω ≢ 1 in it (otherwise π ∼ λ, which is fixed). So ω has order 3 there and 3 ∣ p − 1. For a cubic χ mod p, p = J · J̄ with J and J̄ prime, so π is a unit times J or J̄ (`CubicMatch.split_of_not_fixed`, `assoc_jacobi`, `kappa_J`).

**4.5 Reconstruction of the radicand.** Strong induction on |N(A)| gives (`CubicRecon.reconstruct`)

```latex
A = \omega^{j}\, Y^{3} \prod_{i=1}^{m} q_i J_i, \qquad Y \in R .
```

Units are ±ω^j, and −1 = (−1)³. A fixed prime is removed as a cube π³. At a Jacobi prime J with x = v\_J(A), the class of x mod 3 decides what is removed: J³ if x ≡ 0, qJ = J²J̄ if x ≡ 2, and qJ̄ = JJ̄² if x ≡ 1. Each removal keeps the constraint of §4.3 and lowers |N(A)|. Each factor qJ equals g(χ)³, and ζ₉³ = ω, so (`gauss_realization`, `radicand_cube_root`)

```latex
A = \gamma^{3}, \qquad \gamma = \zeta_9^{\,j}\; Y \prod_{i=1}^{m} g(\chi_i) .
```

**4.6 Cyclotomic containment.** From s³β³ = γ³, the ratio sβ/γ is a cube root of unity, so β = ω^i γ / s. Each factor is cyclotomic: ζ₉ ∈ ℚ(ζ₉), Y, s, ω ∈ ℚ(ζ₃), and each Gauss sum lies in ℚ(ζ\_{3q}), since its terms are cube roots of unity times powers of ζ\_q. Hence K ⊆ ℚ(ω, β) ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_N) (`CubicKW.gaussSum_inCyc`, `cubic_kronecker_weber`). The N produced this way is a multiple of the conductor, not the conductor itself.

## 5. Character recovery and the intrinsic conductor

Once K sits inside some ℚ(ζ\_N), its characters can be read off, and the result does not depend on which N was used. This gives Theorem B.

**5.1 Recovery at one level.** H\_N(K) has index 3, so H\_N(K)^⊥ has three elements, each of order dividing 3. For any nontrivial ψ in it, H\_N(K)^⊥ = {1, ψ, ψ²}. Let Ψ be the primitive character of ψ, at level f = cond(ψ). Mathlib's correspondence between subfields of ℚ(ζ\_N) and character groups, with “conductor divides m” characterizing ℚ(ζ\_m), gives K ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_m) ⇔ f ∣ m for every m ∣ N (`CubicRecovery.le_cyc_iff`, `character_recovery`).

**5.2 Inflation compatibility.** Let N ∣ P with K ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_N). Restricting from ℚ(ζ\_P) to ℚ(ζ\_N) matches reduction (ℤ/P)ˣ → (ℤ/N)ˣ (Mathlib: `galEquivZMod_restrictNormal_apply`). An element fixes K exactly when its restriction does, and every element of Gal(ℚ(ζ\_N)/ℚ) lifts. Hence a character mod N is trivial on H\_N(K) exactly when its inflation to P is trivial on H\_P(K) (`CubicIntrinsic.compat`).

**5.3 Independence of the level.** Compare any level M with the original N through P = N · M. By §5.2, H\_P(K)^⊥ = {1, ψ↑, ψ↑²}, the inflations of the level-N characters. This yields (`CubicIntrinsic.intrinsic_pair`):

- K ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_m) ⇔ f ∣ m, for **every** m ≥ 1. This is divisibility, so f is an invariant of K and not merely a minimum below one chosen N.
- At every level M with K ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_M), the inflation of Ψ is nontrivial and trivial on H\_M(K).
- At every such M, a primitive Φ of level f′ ∣ M whose inflation is nontrivial and trivial on H\_M(K) has f′ = f and Φ ∈ {Ψ, Ψ̄}.

The invariant is the unordered pair {Ψ, Ψ̄}. Choosing Ψ over Ψ̄ is an orientation, not extra arithmetic information.

## 6. The correspondence

The two constructions are inverse to each other, which gives Theorem C. The field attached to Ψ is built at Ψ's exact conductor f; no inflated representative is used to define it.

**6.1 Embedded fields and isomorphism classes.** Let E be an abstract number field, Galois over ℚ. All ℚ-embeddings E → ℂ have the same image. The image of one embedding is a normal subfield of ℂ, and every other embedding maps onto it (Mathlib: `AlgHom.fieldRange_of_normal`). An automorphism of the image only moves elements inside it. Two such fields are isomorphic over ℚ exactly when their images in ℂ are equal (`GaloisEmbedding.fieldRange_eq`, `iso_iff_range_eq`). So isomorphism classes of cyclic cubic fields correspond one-to-one with cyclic cubic subfields of ℂ.

**6.2 Field → pair → field.** Let K be cyclic cubic with intrinsic pair {Ψ, Ψ̄} of conductor f. By §5.3, K ⊆ ℚ(ζ\_f) and Ψ is trivial on H\_f(K). So H\_f(K) ≤ ker Ψ, and K\_Ψ ⊆ K. Both have degree 3, so K\_Ψ = K (`CubicBijection.KΨ_eq_of_mem`, `field_to_char_to_field`).

**6.3 Pair → field → pair.** For primitive cubic Ψ and Ψ′ of conductors f and f′,

```latex
K_{\Psi'} = K_{\Psi} \iff f' = f \ \text{and}\ \Psi' \in \{\Psi, \bar\Psi\} .
```

The forward implication applies the uniqueness of §5.3 to both characters against the same intrinsic pair. The reverse holds because Ψ and Ψ² have the same kernel (`CubicBijection.fiber`, `KΨ_sq`). Characters of different levels have different types in Lean, so they are compared value by value on ℕ. Once f′ = f, the proof turns this into equality of characters.

**6.4 Summary.** The map Ψ ↦ K\_Ψ is onto the cyclic cubic subfields of ℂ, its fibres are exactly the conjugate pairs, and each K\_Ψ has ζ\_{K\_Ψ} = ζ · L(Ψ) · L(Ψ̄) (`CubicBijection.classification`). With §6.1, abstract cyclic cubic fields E ≅ E′ exactly when their characters agree up to conjugation (`abstract_classification`).

## 7. Scope and limitations

The formal statements say exactly what §§1–6 say, and the points below mark where a reader might expect more.

- **Classical content.** The classification, the conductor shapes and the zeta factorization are known results. This document does not claim new mathematics; it claims a complete machine-checked proof by a particular elementary route.
- **Dependence on Mathlib.** The proof uses established Mathlib theorems, not only definitions: Kummer theory for cyclic extensions (`isCyclic_tfae`), the PID property and unit group of ℤ\[ω\], the Galois correspondence, the subfield–character correspondence for ℚ(ζ\_N) with its conductor criterion, and Galois restriction between cyclotomic fields. The formalization is only as trustworthy as those results and Lean's kernel.
- **Packaging of the bijection.** Theorem C is stated as a surjective map whose fibres are exactly the conjugate pairs, plus the abstract-isomorphism version. It is not packaged as a Lean `Equiv` between a quotient type of characters and a type of fields. That packaging would add no mathematical content.
- **Pointwise comparison.** Characters of different levels are compared value by value on ℕ, because their types differ until the levels are shown equal.
- **The level from Theorem A.** The N built in §4.6 is a multiple of the conductor. Exactness comes only afterwards, from §5.
- **A redundant hypothesis.** Some statements take `[FiniteDimensional ℚ K]` in addition to \[K : ℚ\] = 3. Lean needs it before it will accept a statement about ζ\_K; it follows from the degree hypothesis.
- **Region of validity.** The zeta factorization is proved for Re s > 1 only. Nothing here concerns analytic continuation or the zeros of any L-function.
- **Sources.** The References section lists the classical sources and related formal work, and its table separates what is imported from Mathlib from what is formalized here.
